The roads not taken

Which roads has a fleet really driven? 39 million taxi GPS fixes matched to OpenStreetMap with PySpark, and the gaps a data-collection fleet should drive next. The answer: every motorway, but only about half the residential streets with any regularity. The blue lines on the map are the streets no taxi drove in six months.

    How it works

    One PySpark job, from raw trips to coverage, in about a minute and a half on a 12-core laptop.

    1. 🧾Trips into GPS fixesEach trip is one row with its route as a JSON list of points every 15 s. from_json + posexplode turns 800k trips into 39M timestamped fixes across all cores.
    2. 🧹CleaningDuplicate trips dropped. Fixes outside the city removed. A spike (jumps away and straight back at an impossible speed) is removed with a window over each trip, which shuffles the data by trip.
    3. 🔷Matching without a geometric searchRoads are pre-indexed on the H3 grid (cells about 9 m across). Every fix gets its cell, so matching is an equality join. A continuity step fixes single odd matches between two fixes on the same road.
    4. 📊CoveragePer segment: trips, taxis, days, first and last seen. Per road class: kilometres driven once and driven regularly.

    🚧 Limits

    • This run covers the first 800,488 trips of the year-long dataset, July to December 2013.
    • Matching picks the nearest road with a continuity check: 96% of fixes land on the correct street, but only 64% on the exact segment, mostly at junctions and divided roads.
    • Validation uses synthetic drives, which show how the method behaves, not how real drivers do.
    • Taxis follow passengers, not a survey plan, so "never driven" here means "never driven by this fleet".
